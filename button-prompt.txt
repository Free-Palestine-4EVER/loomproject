Extreme macro overhead shot of one single button made entirely of hot
pink wool yarn (#F21C8C), floating alone on a fully transparent
background with no scene, no surface and no backdrop of any kind. Shot
from directly above at 90 degrees, perfectly circular in frame, no
perspective distortion, precisely centred, filling about 80 percent of
the frame.

The button is not plastic — it is a crocheted and wound yarn button.
Real fibre from edge to edge: a dense spiral of fine pink wool yarn
coiled tightly from the outer rim inward, every individual ply and twist
of the strand clearly resolved, thousands of tiny fuzzy fibre ends
catching the light and standing up off the surface in a soft halo. The
wool is deeply saturated hot magenta pink, with lighter blush pink where
the light hits the top of each strand and deep rose shadow in the
valleys between coils. A slight dome to the whole button, softly
stuffed, so the centre sits proud of the rim.

Four small holes are worked through the middle in a square, their edges
bound with neat buttonhole stitching, and cream wool yarn (#EFE7DA) is
threaded through them in a raised crossed X, the cream strand visibly
fuzzy and twisted, its cut ends soft and frayed.

Lit by a large soft overhead diffuser with one gentle key from the upper
left, so the fibre halo rims with light. A whisper of self-shadow
between the coils gives the wool depth — no cast shadow onto anything,
because there is nothing beneath it.

Photoreal macro fibre photography, tack sharp, ultra detailed, every
fibre visible, everything in focus, isolated cutout on transparent
background, 2K.

NOTES
-----
· Ask for a TRANSPARENT PNG. If the model insists on a background, take
  the cleanest one and run remove_background — and ask for flat PURE
  WHITE, never black. A fuzzy fibre halo cut off a dark backdrop leaves
  a dark fringe you cannot clean up.

· Generate 4 and pick for LIGHT DIRECTION first, detail second. The
  card is lit from the upper left; a right-lit button reads as pasted
  on no matter how good the fibre is.

· Keep it at 80 percent of frame, not 95 — the fibre halo needs
  headroom or the fuzz clips at the edges.

· VARIANT — the thread carries the CTA and you drop the SVG arrow
  entirely. Replace the cream-thread paragraph with:

    Cream wool yarn is stitched across the face into a small arrow
    pointing right, the raised stitches forming a clean chevron.

· If you keep an SVG arrow on top instead, ask for the crossed X
  low-contrast so the arrow stays readable over the fibre.
