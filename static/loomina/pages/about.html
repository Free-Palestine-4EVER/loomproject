The Workshop

A furnace that never fully cools.

Our glasshouse sits two streets back from the Grand Canal, in a Murano building that has held a working furnace since before either of us was born. It stays lit between 1,050°C and 1,150°C for the whole working week — dropping it to room temperature and bringing it back up again is what actually shortens a furnace's life, not the blowing itself. Four gaffers rotate through it in relay shifts, because hand-blown glass does not tolerate a tired wrist near the end of a piece.

Every shade, globe and diffuser in the catalogue passes through that furnace at least twice: once to gather and shape, once to re-heat and finish the lip by hand with a wooden paddle called a borsella's cousin, a mazzuoletto. No two are optically identical. We build the metalwork to that tolerance, not the other way around.

Materials

Spun, not stamped.

The metal side happens above a old rope factory in Bushwick, on three manual spinning lathes we bought secondhand from a shuttered lampshade maker in Providence. A flat disc of brushed brass, blackened steel or bronze goes on the lathe, and a spinner presses it into shape against a wooden form with a hand tool, the same way it's been done since the 1920s. Stamping a shade from a die is faster and it shows: the walls thin unevenly and the seam telegraphs through the finish. A spun shade keeps a constant wall thickness end to end, which is also why it holds heat and sound the way our customers say it does.

We finish in five house tones — blackened steel, brushed brass, aged bronze, matte bone and smoked walnut on the wood accents — and we test each one under the fixture's own bulb before it ships, because a finish that looks right cold can go muddy under 2700K.

Why one number

Every Loomina fixture is wound, wired and shipped at 2700K.

It is the warm-white of an old filament bulb — the colour a room looked before daylight LEDs made everything a little colder and a little bluer than it needed to be. We picked it once and we have not revisited it since, so a chandelier from our first year and a sconce that shipped last month still read as the same light in the same room.

Warranty

Five years on everything we build.

Glasswork, wiring, sockets and the spinning or blackening on the metal are covered for five years against manufacturing defects. That does not cover bulbs, ordinary tarnish on unlacquered brass — which we consider a feature, not a fault — or damage from a fixture rated for indoor use being hung outdoors. Email us the order number and a photo and we will tell you within two business days whether it is covered.

Care Guide

Keeping it the way it arrived.

  • Hand-blown glassDust with a dry microfiber cloth only. Glass cleaner leaves a film that dulls under a warm bulb — if it needs more than dust, use lukewarm water and let it air-dry fully before rewiring.
  • Unlacquered brassWill darken and spot over months — this is a real patina, not tarnish gone wrong. Polish only if you want it bright again; a light coat of mineral oil slows the change if you'd rather it stayed close to new.
  • Blackened steelSealed with a hand-rubbed wax finish. Reapply a thin coat of dark paste wax once a year in a dry cloth to keep it from going grey at the edges.
  • BulbsEvery fixture is built for 2700K — using a colder bulb won't damage anything, but it will fight the finish. We list the exact wattage and base on each product page.
