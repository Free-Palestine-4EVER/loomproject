## 1. A website proposal should answer more than “how much?”

### LinkedIn

When two website proposals look similar, the price is rarely the useful thing to compare first.

Ask what is actually included: Who writes or adapts the Arabic content? Who owns the files and domains after launch? Is the site tested on real phones? What happens when the first edit is needed? Is there a clear launch plan?

For businesses in Amman, these details decide whether a website becomes a working part of the business or an expensive PDF that never quite gets finished.

## 2. E-commerce in Jordan is a checkout decision

### LinkedIn

An online store can look excellent and still lose the sale at checkout.

In Jordan, the details are specific: Arabic-first content, a payment approach customers recognise, clear delivery expectations and a cash-on-delivery flow that does not create avoidable friction.

The right setup depends on the business, its operations and its customers. But treating checkout as a final technical step usually means discovering the important questions too late.

## 3. Can customers verify your business in AI-assisted search?

### LinkedIn

People increasingly ask an assistant to narrow down a purchase, compare services or find a business nearby. The useful question for a business is not “how do we trick an AI into recommending us?”

It is: can a person verify who we are when they find us?

That means consistent business details, an accurate Google Business Profile, clear service pages, useful answers to real questions and a site that makes contact information easy to find.

No formatting trick guarantees a recommendation. Clear, current information gives customers and search systems something reliable to work with.
