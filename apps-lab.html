Seven products. Three ways to show them.

The apps section today spends a whole phone card on every product and never once shows an app's icon — the single most recognisable thing each one owns. All three of these lead with the icon, and all three are built to cost the GPU nothing: no canvas, no blur, no idle loops, transform and opacity only. Each section states exactly how many things are moving at any one moment.

01

The shelf

The App Store treatment: the icon is the hero, one line of what it is, one line of what it does, and the store it lives on. All seven products fit in a single screen on desktop, so the section reads as a catalogue of things we have shipped rather than a gallery of screenshots — which is the actual claim being made. It is honest about the set too: the icon is a first-class asset for every product, including TrueSize AR, which has no screenshot at all. What it gives up is the screenshots themselves — nobody sees a real interface here, so the "these are not mockups" line has to move elsewhere or go.

Animation cost: zero at rest. Nothing moves until a pointer lands on a card, and then exactly one card responds — a 3px lift and a rotate on its icon. No images beyond the one 5 KB KwaKwa icon; the other six icons are CSS gradients.

02

The stage

One phone, centre stage, and a rail of icons that switches what is on it. Instead of seven phones competing on the page, there is a single device and the visitor drives it — which makes the screenshots bigger and far more legible than the current grid can ever be, and it gives the icons a real job as navigation rather than decoration. Depth over breadth: it is the strongest way to actually look at a product, and the weakest at conveying "we have shipped seven of these", because six of them are 58px squares at any given moment. TrueSize AR, which has no capture, gets a proper stated panel rather than an empty frame.

Animation cost: one element at a time. Switching runs a single 380ms opacity crossfade between two stacked <img> tags — no layout, no repaint of the page. All seven screenshots are already in the DOM and decoded, so switching costs nothing after the first load. At rest, nothing is animating.

03

The index

The one that treats the products as a list of things, split by where they run — six iOS apps in one block, the web app in its own. Names set large in Clash Display, a 38px icon in the margin, a one-line description, the store badge on the right; hovering or tapping a row loads it into the sticky preview at the side. It is the most scannable of the three and by far the shortest section on the page, so it works if the apps are a credential rather than the main event. Its weakness is the same as its strength: it is quiet, and a visitor skimming fast may read it as a footer list instead of seven real products.

Animation cost: two properties, one row. A hovered row shifts 10px of padding and its wash fades in; the preview swaps one image's opacity. Nothing loops, nothing moves at rest, and the preview is the only image over 40 KB on screen at once.
