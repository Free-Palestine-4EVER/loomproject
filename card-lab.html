Same tile. Five strips.

The card, the photograph and the four-yarn rail are exactly what the site ships today — nothing about them moves. Only the label strip and its button change, and every one of the five is built to land standing still: the detail is in the material, not in a hover. Hover only nudges.

01

Woven tape

The flat cream pill becomes an actual length of woven ribbon — four yarn threads run through the weft, a warp tooth runs the other way, and both selvedge edges are stitched in the card's own dye. The button is a wound ball of magenta yarn with a lit top-left and a loose tail curling up into the photograph. Nothing here needs a cursor to be seen.

02

Spool tab

The strip is a spool lying on its side: a turned wooden end cap on the left, a cream barrel with the yarn visibly wound around it, and the right flange dyed magenta as the CTA. It runs off the left edge of the card the way the tag on a real bolt of fabric does, and a single thread feeds up out of it into the image. The loudest of the five.

03

Cross-stitch plate

The quietest, and the closest to what you have. Dark glass instead of cream, so the photograph keeps going underneath it — and along the top edge a row of hand cross-stitches in the card's dye, made from two crossing dash runs. A small caps line rides above the plate. The button sits in an embroidery hoop — a hairline ring plus a soft dye halo.

04

Yarn ball

The one that reads from across the room. The button breaks out of the strip entirely — a 64px knitted ball hanging off the bottom-right corner, cross-wound in three directions with a real specular highlight, trailing a strand back along the tape. On the left, the strip is capped with a chip of this card's yarn, so the grid doubles as a colour chart.

05

Swatch tag

The most editorial. The strip is a fabric-sample tag — punched hanging hole with an eyelet on the left, a perforated tear-off edge running along the whole top, a dye chip beside a small-caps category line, and the service name under it. The CTA is a stamped squircle with a bevel rather than a flat circle. Gives every tile two lines of information instead of one.

06

Twenty buttons

The cross-stitch plate from 03, twenty times. The card, the plate and the label never change — only the object on the right does, and no two are the same thing: a coil, a spool, a pom-pom, a braid, a skein, a thimble, a zip pull, a tape measure. Every one animates on its own with no cursor near it. All twenty are transform, opacity or background-position only.
